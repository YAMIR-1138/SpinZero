How do you balance a centrifuge with an odd number of tubes?

A centrifuge rotor is balanced when the centre of mass of the loaded tubes sits on the spin axis. Model each of the n slots as a point on the unit circle, e2πij/n. A set of k tubes is balanced exactly when those points add up to zero – a vanishing sum of roots of unity.

Pairs of opposite tubes, equilateral triangles and other regular polygons all cancel out, and so does any combination of them that doesn’t overlap. So an odd count is often fine: 5 tubes in a 12‑slot rotor balance as a triangle plus one opposite pair – but 5 tubes in a 6‑slot rotor never can.

When is a balanced arrangement possible?

By the Lam–Leung theorem, k tubes can be balanced in an n-slot rotor if and only if both k and n − k can be written as sums of prime factors of n. For example, in a 12‑slot rotor (prime factors 2 and 3) every tube count except 1 and 11 can be balanced, while in a 6‑slot rotor 1 and 5 tubes are impossible. ΣpinZero checks this instantly and then builds an arrangement for you.

Modes

  • Cycle Decomposition – builds a balanced layout from non-overlapping regular polygons; fast for any rotor size.
  • Elegant / Cursed / Random – search every arrangement (rotors up to 24 slots) for the most symmetric, the most chaotic-looking, or any balanced layout.
  • Evil – the arrangement that is almost balanced: the smallest possible non-zero imbalance.
  • Heuristic Random – a random balanced layout for large rotors.
  • Manual – click slots to check your own loading pattern before you hit start.

Always follow your centrifuge manufacturer’s instructions: tubes must also be filled to equal mass, and some rotors have their own loading rules.
